= Frequently Asked Questions

== How do I register for the ITC Internship class?

First, you need to find an IT position you can use for internship credit. See xref:_how_do_i_find_an_internship_position[How do I find an internship position?] below and Step 1 of the "ITC Internship Course Enrollment Steps" for details. Once you have secured a position, see the section “ITC Internship Course Enrollment Steps” in the xref:guidelines-policies.adoc[Guidelines and Policies] section for detailed registration steps.

The ITC Internship course is not a course where you register and then find or get placed at an internship. Instead, you take this course to receive credit for internship work experience; therefore, you must have an accepted position before registering. This is why instructor approval is required to register.

The ITC Internship course is offered every term and, although it only shows a limited number of seats, the course will not fill up as we will expand the course as needed. You can also register for this course even after a term starts so there is not a rush to register for the course. See FAQ #6 for more details.

== When am I ready to take the internship course?

We normally recommend that you start looking for internship positions anytime after you have completed the ITC 2000 PC Hardware & Software course. The ITC Internship course is offered every term (including summer) and you can also register for this course even after a term starts so as soon as you are hired for a position and have approval from the ITC internship supervisor you can start.

== How do I find an internship position?

You will find your internship position through the same search strategies used to find any IT role. IHCC does not directly place students in internships; you are responsible for seeking and securing a suitable position. We strongly recommend working with the https://www.inverhills.edu/careerdevelopment[IHCC Career Development Center] to review your resume, practice interview skills, and refine your search strategy. If you have not yet completed it taking the ITC-2100: IT Career Success Skills and Strategies class (typically offered in the Spring) can also assist you in providing some IT specific job search, application, and interview advice.

It's easier to get hired if you have previous experience in IT. However, we realize this is not normally the case for students! Especially if you do not have previous experience, you will probably find it easier to get hired if you have passed professional certification exams so that employers know you have demonstrated knowledge, skills, and abilities. The CompTIA A+ professional certification is a good place to start. If you've taken the ITC PC Hardware & Software course, you should be well on your way to passing that certification. We suggest contacting your instructor for advice and taking practice exams to prepare. If you already have the CompTIA A+ and have completed our CCNA courses, passing the official CCNA exam is another great way to prove your technical capabilities.

=== What kinds of positions count?

In the IT field, very few entry-level roles are explicitly advertised as "internships." *Most standard, paid IT jobs qualify for internship credit as long as you are able to meet all the requirements laid out in our internship handbook.*

* *Common Roles:* Most students securing their first IT position land in roles like _IT Helpdesk, Service Desk Analyst, or Desktop Support._
* *Internal vs. External Support:* Positions providing *internal support* (supporting a company’s own employees) typically work best because they allow opportunities for small project work or job-shadowing with higher-tier IT staff. *External support roles* (supporting outside customers or call centers) often lack these cross-training opportunities.
+
--
NOTE: MSP (Managed Service Provider) entry roles, even when client-facing, often act like internal support and provide strong broad-spectrum technical exposure.
--
* *Requirements Check:* See the Internship Guidelines and Step 1 of the _ITC Internship Course Enrollment Steps_ later in this handbook for detailed criteria. Contact your IHCC ITC internship supervisor if you are unsure whether a specific position qualifies.

=== Where should I look for a job?

. *Local & Regional Job Board Searches:*
** *Dice.com:* Tech-focused job board with strong Twin Cities listings.
** *ZipRecruiter:* Popular job posting board used by many regional employers.
** *LinkedIn & Indeed:* Set your location search radius to Minneapolis–St. Paul (or within your commuting distance, e.g., 25–30 miles of home).
** *Handshake:* Accessible through IHCC Career Development to find employers specifically recruiting college talent.
. *Tech Staffing Agencies:*
+
--
A large percentage of entry-level IT roles in the MSP metro area are contract-to-hire positions filled through IT staffing firms (such as _Robert Half, Apex Systems, Insight Global, Kforce, or TekSystems_).
--
** *Pros:* They can get your resume in front of hiring managers quickly and offer valuable interview coaching.
** *Cons:* Be mindful of your time, ensure the recruiter is matching you with roles aligned with your actual skill level and not just trying to fill interview quotas. Also make sure the job opportunities they are presenting you with are interesting and appropriate for your skills.
+
--
NOTE: A 3- to 6-month contract position is a fantastic way to gain early experience and counts toward internship requirements.
--
. *Networking in the Twin Cities:*
+
--
TIP: Networking remains the single most effective way to land any job whether an IT position or something else.
--
** Talk to family, friends, former coworkers, and classmates to let them know you are seeking an entry-level IT role.
** Explore local tech networks like https://www.hdimn.com[HDI Minnesota], https://mntech.org/[Minnesota Tech Association (MnTech)], local LinkedIn tech groups, or the https://discord.gg/3srdSetPuP[MSP Tech Discord community].
** Attend campus career fairs hosted by IHCC or regional college networks.

=== Search Terms to Try

Job titles in IT are not standardized. A position with the exact same duties might be called _Service Desk Analyst I_ at one company and _PC Technician_ at another. Try combining these keywords in your searches:

* *Role Titles:* _Helpdesk_, _Service Desk_, _IT Support Specialist_, _Desktop Support_, _PC Technician_, _Support Analyst_, _ITSM / ITIL_, _NOC Technician_
* *Certification / Skill Keywords:* _CompTIA A+_, _Network+_, _Active Directory_, _Entra ID_, _Office 365 / M365_, _Windows 11_, _Ticketing System_

TIP: Always read the full job description before skipping a posting, a job title that sounds overly specialized or unfamiliar might actually be an entry-level support role underneath!

== What if I am unable to find a position titled "intern" or described as an internship?

No problem! We realize there are very few positions advertised specifically as internships in the IT field. Most IT jobs can be used for credit in the internship course. See details elsewhere in this handbook for specific requirements for positions. For advice on looking for a position see the above question.

== What about jobs that list a requirement for a degree or experience?

You are encouraged to apply for jobs you think you're qualified for even if they list a degree (2-year or 4-year) or experience as a requirement which you don't have yet. In many cases, these requirements are listed in the hopes of finding someone with those exact credentials, but they are often preferences rather than strict requirements. Most entry-level helpdesk positions do not strictly require a completed degree to start. Having certifications (such as CompTIA A+ or CCNA) on your resume demonstrates that you have the required knowledge, making employer "requirements" negotiable.

== What if I don't have an internship position when classes start for the term or I won't have 192 hours done by the end of the term?

This is why we require you have a position and receive approval before you are allowed to register for the internship course. Keep looking for a position. Internships are not like regular classes, we can register you for the internship course at any time during the semester, you do not need to register before the semester starts. As soon as you find and are hired for a position you can contact the ITC internship supervisor to get registered for the class even if it's the middle of the semester.

We will normally register you for the course during the semester where the majority of your hours will be completed. This may mean that you start working at your internship even before you are registered for the course, that's OK as long as you have cleared it with the ITC internship supervisor and have completed all the assignments as described in the xref:itc-2189:guidelines-policies.adoc[Guidelines and Policies] and xref:itc-2189:syllabus.adoc[Syllabus] portions of the handbook.

If you get to the end of a semester which you are registered for the internship course and have been working at an internship but don't have all your 192 hours or assignments completed yet you should contact your ITC internship supervisor. Our normal process is for you to request an "Incomplete" grade for the internship course, due to campus policy you must request and agree to the incomplete. You can then finish your hours and/or assignments and work with your ITC internship supervisor to get your final grade submitted and the course completed.

If you are unable to complete the internship within one semester after the semester in which you are registered for the internship you will fail the internship course and need to re-take it to complete the internship requirement.

== Can I get credit for work I did in the past? What if I worked somewhere last summer and want to get internship credit for that work?

Sorry, no. You must contact the ITC internship supervisor, register for the class, and complete some of the assignments before you can begin counting your hours towards the internship. You then must do other assignments during the course of the 192 hours, and still others after the internship is over. You cannot do these assignments after the fact and need to collaborate with the ITC internship supervisor to get approval for your internship before you can count work towards the internship.

== What if I already have a job and am unable to take an internship?

Our goal with the internship course is to launch or propel your career in IT so it is important to get real-world work experience in the IT field.

If you already work in the IT area (see the Internship Guidelines for details on what constitutes the IT area) it is possible that your existing job may be able to count for the internship. In this case you should read through the requirements in this handbook and then contact the ITC internship supervisor to discuss your situation and request approval to use your current job.

If you do not currently work in the IT field you probably will not be able to count your current job for the internship and will need to find an internship which meets the requirements in this handbook. We do normally require that internships are paid (more information later in this handbook) so the internship course may provide an opportunity to leave your current position and begin your IT career.

Finally, there is much flexibility in the internship program, while many students work 20-40 hours a week during their internship you don't have to do this, it's possible to complete the internship (over a longer period of time) working 5-10 hours a week if you have another job which you need to keep.
